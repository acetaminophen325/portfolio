Agentic genomics pipeline

An always-on agentic pipeline running on an NVIDIA DGX Spark. Bioinformatics steps are wrapped as MCP tools, local models are served with vLLM and Ollama behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and an agent orchestrates the end-to-end genomics workflow with quality gates, retries, and monitoring. I own the stack from containers and GPU memory budgeting up through the agent and tool layer.

Campus Navigator

A live class finder for the UCI campus. It follows your location and the clock, ranks the classes meeting near you right now by walking distance and time, draws the A* walking route to any of them over real OpenStreetMap paths, and tells you whether you can make it before it starts. Plus BM25 keyword search and a simulation mode for any place and time.

Group final project; nearly all of it is mine, with a teammate on the schedule scraper. Repo: campus-navigator.

BME capstone · BioEngine Symposium

Earicular: auricular reconstruction capstone

Two contributions to an ear reconstruction capstone. I built the team's project website solo (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, deployed over SFTP). On the engineering side, I developed the MATLAB biomechanical analysis pipeline: Instron force-displacement data converted to engineering stress-strain, extracting ultimate tensile strength, strain at failure, resilience, toughness, and Young's modulus by linear regression.

The pipeline benchmarked an allograft against published autologous cartilage values (Young's modulus 13.5 ± 9.9 MPa, UTS 4.9 ± 3.2 MPa, within physiological range). Presented at the BioEngine Symposium.

Website repo is fully mine: earicular-website, live at earicular.uci.design.

>about

Two degrees, one direction

I studied two things at once on purpose. Computer Science, specialized in Intelligent Systems, taught me to build and serve machine learning systems. Biomedical Engineering taught me what those systems are for: real tissue, real assays, real clinical constraints. Plenty of ML work treats biology as a dataset, and plenty of biology treats ML as a black box. I live in the gap, and it's the most exciting place I know.

Right now I'm a research assistant in the Duran Computational Lab at UC Riverside, building automated, reproducible genomics and structure-prediction workflows on GPU/HPC infrastructure, with two manuscripts in preparation. I care about work that holds up: pipelines that rerun cleanly, results that survive scrutiny, and tools that lab members can pick up without me in the room.

The rest of my background keeps me grounded in the physical side of the field. I've debugged embedded firmware as a design engineering intern, learned FDA design controls, and put in bench time with PCR, gel electrophoresis, and mammalian cell culture. When I build a model for biology, I know what the data cost to produce, and I want the tools I ship to be worthy of it.
